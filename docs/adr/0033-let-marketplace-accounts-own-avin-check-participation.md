# Let marketplace accounts own Avin Check participation

Existing Buyer and Seller accounts may own Protection Provider Applications and submit Public Risk Reports through their normal Avin session while remaining separate marketplace account types. Provider approval grants access to Provider capabilities and the private workspace without creating or switching to a `PROVIDER` account role, and reporting requires no second email OTP; reporter identity remains private outside authorized review. This supersedes only the distinct Provider account and authentication decision in ADR-0027 while preserving Admin-only publication, immutable Provider profile versions, and the Avin Check bounded context.

Seller approval is not required to apply, but a locked account cannot apply or enter the workspace. Confirmed marketplace fraud blocks a new application and requires review of an existing Provider, whereas ordinary Seller suspension does not automatically change Provider standing. Because this decision is being made before production Provider identities exist, the separate Provider auth surface and role will be removed as a clean cutover without legacy account relinking or data backfill.

One verified person or business may hold only one Provider standing even if it controls separate Buyer and Seller accounts; duplicate identity evidence is rejected during review. Provider profiles do not automatically disclose or link the owning account's marketplace role or Store. Provider application and workspace surfaces live inside Avin Check under the normal marketplace layout and session, while Public Risk Reports are account-owned, use account-derived private identity, and appear in a private Risk Report Workspace without exposing Admin-only material.

Correctable Provider application problems use `CHANGES_REQUESTED`; terminal `REJECTED` is reserved for fraud, impersonation, or a definitive policy prohibition. Drafting and reading use the ordinary authenticated session, but submitting an application, submitting a public-profile revision, and requesting Bond Withdrawal require 2FA. If the owner loses access, normal account recovery is preferred; an Admin may relink the Provider record only to another Buyer or Seller account proven to represent the same verified identity, with an immutable audit trail, and may never transfer the standing to a different identity.

Seller Enforcement does not by itself remove access to Public Risk Reporting while the account remains able to authenticate, although authorized moderators see the relevant enforcement context and reporting remains rate-limited and moderated. Account deletion removes report drafts; submitted and published reports retain a private reporter-identity snapshot and their moderation history under the applicable audit-retention policy, while the deleted account loses workspace access.

A Provider Owner may report itself or another Provider after declaring the relationship, which remains private and is shown to moderators as a conflict signal. Public warnings never expose the reporter's name, marketplace role, contact details, or Provider relationship and may identify the source only as an authenticated Avin user. Reporting is rate-limited by account and IP, with duplicate detection that still permits moderators to merge reports or accept new evidence, and status notifications use in-app delivery plus the account's verified email.

Risk Report Correction Requests also require a normal Buyer or Seller account. A person without an account first follows normal registration and becomes a Buyer. The original reporter acts through its workspace; a reported subject or authorized representative signs in and supplies evidence of identity ownership or authority to act. Avin provides no email-OTP-only correction path, and authentication alone is not treated as proof of ownership. A valid session is sufficient for reports and correction requests without 2FA; 2FA remains reserved for the sensitive Provider operations defined above. Unauthenticated visits to report, application, workspace, or correction surfaces redirect through normal Avin login with a return URL.

A reporter may delete a draft, but after submission may only request withdrawal; an Admin may preserve and continue the review when public safety requires it, and a published warning is never deleted directly by its reporter. Duplicate reports remain separately owned and private, while an Admin may associate them with a single public warning without exposing reporters to one another. The clean development cutover resets obsolete Provider-auth and reporter-OTP development data and introduces no backfill, compatibility mode, or legacy relinking logic.
